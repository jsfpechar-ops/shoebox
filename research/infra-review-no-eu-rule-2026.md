# Infra review without the EU-only rule: does dropping it save money or effort?

**As of:** 2026-10-18. **Author:** Fable 5.1 (research analyst). **Trigger:** owner said "if it saves money and makes it more efficient, feel free to ignore EU processing and find the best solution." **Method:** WebFetch/WebSearch of vendor pricing and docs, access date 2026-10-18. Labels: **[O]** observed on a primary source · **[E]** estimate or inference · **[?]** not confirmed. Prior work: `infra-ecosystems-2026.md`, `infra-review-fable-2026.md`. LLM = Anthropic API directly (decisions 2026-10-18), identical in every option below, so LLM spend is excluded from all tables.

## 0. Recommendation

**Keep the data in the EU; the saving from leaving is close to zero.** The region choice is not where the money is. Supabase, SES, Cloudflare Workers/R2, Vercel, Railway and Neon publish one price for every region [O, §2]; the only region-priced piece of the current stack is Cloud Run, and its cheaper "Tier 1" rate is available inside the EU (`europe-west1` Belgium, `europe-west4` Netherlands) as well as in the US, while Frankfurt (`europe-west3`) is Tier 2 (+20 %) [O]. So the one real fix is **move Cloud Run from `europe-west3` to `europe-west1`** before the first deploy: it saves ~$12/month at 100 orgs and ~$100/month at 10,000, costs 10 ms of latency for Czech users, changes nothing legally, and does not need the rule waived. What would actually cut the bill is a **platform** change, not a region change: Cloudflare-first (−45 %) or Hetzner self-hosted (−75 %), and both are also EU-locatable. Their savings at 100–1,000 orgs are $40–150/month, bought with new runtimes, beta products or self-managed Postgres, which is a bad trade for a one-person team in Phase 0. Meanwhile moving Czech tax documents and rodná čísla to US storage removes the one answer ("data jsou v EU") that Czech accountants will ask about, and the EU–US DPF is under appeal at the CJEU [O, §4]. **Proposed owner decision:** reword rule 4 from "EU regions only" to "EU regions by default; a non-EU processor needs a decision line plus SCC/DPF, sub-processor entry and privacy-notice line; the database and document storage stay in the EU", pin Cloud Run to `europe-west1`, and set a dated revisit of Cloudflare-first at ~1,000 orgs. Nothing else in the stack changes.

## 4. Does dropping EU-only save money or effort? Barely.

1. **Region is not a price lever in this stack.** Supabase, SES, Cloudflare, Vercel, Railway, Neon: one price everywhere [O, §2]. Cloud Run is the only item with a geographic tier, and the saving (20 %) is available in Belgium or the Netherlands, both EU [O]. A2 (US regions) and A1 (EU Tier 1) cost the same to the dollar. The LLM, the one place a 10 % EU premium existed, has already been moved by the 2026-10-18 decision.
2. **The cheap options are cheap because of the platform, and they are EU-locatable anyway.** Hetzner is German. Fly has `fra`/`ams`/`waw`. R2 has an `eu` jurisdiction. Cloudflare compute is the only place where "ignore EU" buys anything (no region pin needed), and that is a compliance simplification, not a price cut.
3. **Absolute numbers.** At 100 orgs the best non-rewrite saving (B) is ~$45/month; at 1,000 orgs ~$115/month, against revenue of roughly 1,000 × 300 CZK ≈ $12k/month [E]. Infra is 2 % of revenue at 1,000 orgs under A, 1 % under B. The LLM bill ($1k–3k at 1,000 orgs per the prior review) dwarfs either.
4. **Effort goes the wrong way.** Every option cheaper than A adds founder days (B: OpenNext + beta Containers; D: Fly ops; F: 15–25 days/year of database operations). At a founder rate of even $50/hour, F's $70/month saving is paid back by its extra effort in the first week of each year.
5. **Transfer burden is not removed by leaving the EU; it gets heavier.** GDPR applies to Czech customers' data wherever it sits. Today the transfers are secondary (support access, LLM calls, CDN). Under A2 the primary copy of tax documents with rodná čísla would be in the US: Art. 46 SCCs plus a transfer impact assessment, a harder DPIA (22 L-03), and the EU–US DPF being appealed before the CJEU (Latombe, General Court dismissed 2025-09-03, appeal pending) [O] https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/framework-intact-for-now-eu-us-data-flows-dodge-another-bullet. Note that Supabase's processor entity is Supabase Pte. Ltd (Singapore) with SCCs in the DPA even for `eu-central-1` [O] https://supabase.com/legal/dpa, so the paperwork exists either way; the difference is what a Czech accountant hears when they ask where the invoices live.
6. **Latency.** A US database behind an EU or edge web tier is the worst layout (every SSR page pays ~100 ms × round trips). If anything ever moves to the US, web and DB move together, and Czech users pay ~0.3–0.5 s per page [E]. A1 costs +10 ms, invisible.

**Honest answer:** no, dropping the rule does not save money or effort in Phase 0 or at 1,000 orgs. The one money-saving action it uncovered (Cloud Run Tier 1) does not need the rule dropped.
